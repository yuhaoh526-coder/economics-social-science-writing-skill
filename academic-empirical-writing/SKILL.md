# Academic Empirical Writing

Use this skill when the user asks to compare, review, or rewrite the empirical-results prose of a Chinese geography, regional science, economic geography, or related quantitative paper, especially when the target journal favors long, information-dense paragraphs.

## Core objective

Improve the explanatory force of the empirical prose without changing the reported results, sample, method, or target-journal style. Preserve long paragraphs when that is the journal convention. Do not solve weak writing by mechanically adding subheadings or splitting every result into a separate paragraph.

The central transformation is:

> reported number or map observation -> structural judgment -> spatial role or stage process -> evidence-based interpretation

Keep the inference boundary clear through accurate terminology; it need not become a disclaimer at the end of every paragraph.

The prose should answer not only “what changed?” but also “what spatial structure does this reveal?”, “what role does a city, region, or block play?”, and “what process is consistent with the sequence of results?”.

## Before rewriting

1. Read the whole empirical section and the relevant tables/figures before editing a paragraph.
2. Identify the target journal's preferred density, paragraph length, terminology, and order of evidence.
3. Distinguish three kinds of claims:
   - directly supported by an indicator or coefficient;
   - a plausible interpretation of a spatial or temporal pattern;
   - a causal or directional claim that the design cannot identify.
4. Check every reported coefficient, year, city, percentage, significance mark, and trend against the table or figure. Never invent a trend to make the prose stronger.
5. Compare reference papers by sentence function, not by copying their wording: opening judgment, evidence sequence, interpretation, mechanism label, and paragraph closure.
6. Evaluate reviewer or colleague suggestions against the actual text, data, method, and journal style. Do not automatically agree. State whether a suggested change improves clarity, changes the meaning, or is unnecessary. Apply this same scrutiny to earlier assistant suggestions.

## Preferred paragraph architecture

Keep the journal's dense paragraph style, but give each paragraph an internal progression:

1. Open with a concise overall judgment.
2. Present the most diagnostic evidence, usually the key trend or contrast before secondary numbers.
3. Use representative cities, regions, or blocks to substantiate the judgment rather than listing every observation without a role.
4. Explain what the pattern means for spatial organization, network function, or stage evolution.
5. Close with a compact synthesis that links to the next subsection.

Use transitions such as “总体来看”“与此同时”“相较之下”“由此可见” only when they mark a real logical shift. Avoid ending a paragraph with a list of coefficients or a generic sentence such as “不同因素存在差异”. Name the structure that the evidence reveals.

## Section-specific guidance

### Spatial distribution and standard-deviation ellipses

- Convert year-by-year map description into a stage process: initial concentration, expansion or diffusion, and later persistence or reorganization.
- Distinguish city-level relative dispersion within each upstream/midstream/downstream region from differences between regional means. Name the comparison units explicitly. A declining coefficient of variation supports reduced relative dispersion (or descriptive sigma convergence), not a general claim of dynamic or beta convergence; “收敛” is not categorically forbidden.
- Give an explicit baseline for a period-end mean or gap. A downstream mean already exceeding other regions at baseline supports persistent overall advantage, not a claim that the advantage newly spread from a few cities to the whole region.
- Treat city names as evidence for spatial roles: downstream cluster, upstream dual core, multi-center support, peripheral catch-up, or core-periphery contrast.
- For ellipses, combine centroid, axis, area, and flatness into a spatial-organization judgment. Do not interpret a tiny parameter change as a major relocation. Put detailed coordinate ranges in the table when the prose only needs a location and movement direction. Specify whose spatial distribution expands or contracts and along which axis. A stable centroid alone does not establish unchanged regional shares.
- End with a regional comparison, such as internal adjustment in the upper reaches, multi-point support in the middle reaches, and axial extension downstream, only when supported by the reported parameters.

### Network topology

- Explicitly distinguish global reachability from direct density: a network can be fully reachable while direct links remain sparse.
- Explain combinations of density, efficiency, clustering, path length, diameter, and small-world quotient rather than interpreting each statistic in isolation.
- When clustering and average path length both rise, describe stronger local clustering alongside longer average shortest paths. Explain small-world comparison and cross-time change directly, rather than writing “二者并不矛盾”. The small-world quotient alone does not prove that path length is shorter than the random benchmark.
- A zero hierarchy index concerns reachability hierarchy, not equality of city roles. Sparse density plus full reachability implies many pairs require indirect paths, without proving a special intermediary mechanism.
- Use “potential association”, “network condition”, or “structural basis” for an estimated gravity network. Do not claim observed technology, capital, or factor flows unless the data measure them.

### Node centrality

Translate rankings into node roles: receiving center, outward sender, dual-function or composite node, intermediary, or weakly connected periphery. Explain in-degree/out-degree centralization as concentration of receiving versus sending functions. Highlight meaningful mismatches between a city's own development level and its network position. Do not equate high centrality with actual resource absorption without direct flow data.

### CONCOR/block analysis

Explain block roles, not only block numbers. Use functional labels supported by the table, such as internally concentrated, external-receiving, cross-block exporting, or mixed-function. Compare internal-link proportions with expected proportions and external receiving/outgoing counts. If blocks are clustered independently for each year, do not treat the same block number as the same enduring group unless a cross-year matching rule is documented.

### QAP and MRQAP

- State that QAP correlation is a bivariate matrix association and MRQAP is a conditional association after controlling variables in the same model.
- For absolute-difference matrices, a positive coefficient means greater city-pair difference is associated with a higher probability of a network tie; it does not identify which city sends or receives, nor the direction of factor transfer.
- Keep “association”, “correspondence”, “may reflect”, and “is consistent with” for interpretation. Reserve “causes”, “leads to”, “drives”, or “verifies a hypothesis” for designs that identify causality.
- Explain stage changes in relative association: a coefficient can decline while the factor remains important; a factor can be significant in correlation but not in MRQAP because it shares information with other dimensions.
- Compare R² across separately specified models descriptively only. Do not present a higher R² as proof of stronger causal influence or policy effectiveness.
- Give unusual results an interpretation: sign reversals, late significance, a coefficient dropping at the end, or a high bivariate correlation paired with a small conditional coefficient.
- Close with a synthesis of the evolving association structure. Explain absolute-difference directionality and model boundaries once at a suitable methods, subsection, or limitations location; repeat only when a particular result would otherwise be misread.
- Do not replace “可能形成合作空间” with “形成合作空间”, or assert observed orders, technical services, complementarity, or matching merely to remove hedging. Retain a qualified mechanism interpretation or return to what the coefficients directly show. Variable overlap is a possible explanation of significance changes, not an established diagnosis without evidence.

## Style constraints

- Preserve the target journal's long-paragraph style unless the user explicitly requests restructuring.
- Prefer a strong opening judgment over a chronological list of observations.
- Use data selectively: retain values that establish the trend, contrast, or turning point; avoid repeating every number when the table already contains it.
- Make mechanisms concrete: connect digitalization to technology application and information coordination; high-end development to R&D, conversion, and specialized production; industrial structure to production-chain roles; openness to external-market participation.
- Do not add rhetorical labels such as “siphon effect” or “curve-too-high-and-few-companions” unless the evidence and method support the label and the user wants that style.
- Do not weaken every sentence with “possibly”. Use direct language for descriptive findings and qualified language only for interpretations.

## Direct prose without repeated defensive qualifications

- Lead with the finding. Avoid repeatedly ending empirical paragraphs with “不能直接解释为”“尚不足以确认”“不能据此判断”“并不意味着” or “仍有待检验”. Remove a redundant disclaimer or state the supported scope positively; keep necessary distinctions available in methods or limitations.
- Judge function, not keywords. “但”“尚未”“并不”“可能” are not banned. Contrasts such as higher local clustering alongside longer paths, or high development levels alongside modest centrality, can be substantive findings.
- Do not remove a necessary hedge from an untested mechanism to make the prose sound confident. Use direct statements for observations, qualified language for interpretations, and no causal claim beyond the design.
- Prefer “整体重心和主导方向基本稳定” over repeated “尚未改变/尚未引起转移” when stability is the actual finding. Do not replace every negative sentence with an empty “相联系/相一致”. State the variables, direction, period, and comparison where possible.

## Subjects, wording, and evidence-to-meaning links

- Keep the subject and referent explicit, especially after semicolons and long numerical clauses. Replace ambiguous “其”“前者/后者”“区域内” with the relevant city group, regional interior, distribution, or model when needed; these pronouns are acceptable when their referents are clear.
- After an index threshold, ranking, or parameter change, explain its supported spatial or structural meaning. Avoid adding another list of cities as the only follow-up. Do not infer continuous clustering or diffusion from several city values alone.
- Prefer concrete descriptions to compressed or inflated expressions: “高值城市分布范围扩大” instead of “空间提升”; “相对集中分布” instead of unsupported “连片集中”; “回归系数最高” instead of vague “居首”; “两类模型的R²差值缩小” instead of unspecified “差距收敛”. These are contextual preferences, not universal word bans.
- State both the explanatory variable and the outcome. For QAP, “人本化城际差异与网络联系显著正相关” is clearer than “人本化正向赋能”. Distinguish dimension scores, difference matrices, correlations, and regression coefficients throughout.
- “相呼应” may be vague; specify whether results share a direction, occur simultaneously, or support the same structural interpretation. “相一致” also needs an actual evidential connection and is not a substitute for verification.
- Avoid abstract setup such as “以评价体系为研究边界” when the concrete classification suffices: identify dimensions within the index and regional conditions outside it. Preserve matrix symbols, citations, and the study's exact variable definitions.
- When administrative rank is unnecessary to the sentence, use “110个城市” in this manuscript; document sample eligibility accurately where it matters. Do not replace it with “110个地级市” when municipalities are included, or turn this manuscript choice into a universal sampling rule.

## Compression and paragraph density

- Scale cuts to the requested amount. For “缩减一行”“稍微缩一点”, remove a redundant clause or repeated conclusion first, preserving key numbers, interpretation, and paragraph structure. Do not rewrite into a substantially shorter abstract-style paragraph. Exact line reduction depends on document layout.
- Keep representative early-year evidence and explicit period-end comparisons; avoid spending most of a stage paragraph listing baseline cities. Reduce repeated numerical narration and repeated “总体来看” summaries before deleting analytical content.
- When editing an abstract, prioritize the central spatial pattern, network structure, and most diagnostic coefficient changes; move exhaustive dimension trajectories to the results section. Do not invent a journal word limit without checking its guidance.

## Reusable review output

When asked for advice rather than a full rewrite, organize feedback by subsection and identify:

1. what the paragraph currently reports;
2. what structural judgment is missing;
3. how to reorder evidence without changing the paragraph format;
4. one or two sentences that can replace the weak transition or closing;
5. any method or inference boundary that must be stated.

When asked to rewrite, return a polished replacement passage and briefly state the central improvement. Do not alter numerical results without flagging the discrepancy. When reviewing by subsection, use the manuscript’s actual heading names and numbering; do not assign a new numbered subsection to an unnumbered paragraph. Distinguish issues in the supplied original from those remaining in an edited version. If document edits with comments are requested, retain each changed source passage and its specific reason in the corresponding comment; preserve equations, citation formatting, headings, and non-text objects using the document workflow.
